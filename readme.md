# Radar Movable Target Motion Estimation Based on Generalized Radon-Fourier Transform [with MATLAB]

The range to a radar maneuvering target is typically modeled as a multivariate function of its illumination time and multiple motion parameters. This multivariate range function incorporates the modulation of the envelope and phase of the coherent radar target echo, providing a foundation for the estimation of the target's motion. This paper derives maximum likelihood estimators (MLE) for the estimation of maneuvering target motion based on envelope and phase joint measurements, pure phase measurements, and pure phase measurements under high signal-to-noise ratio conditions. The results indicate that the proposed MLE is equivalent to the generalized Radon-Fourier transform (GRFT), generalized Radon transform (GRT), and generalized Fourier transform (GFT), respectively, with their respective output maxima. Furthermore, by approximating the range function with higher order polynomials, inherent precision limitations, namely the Cramer-Rao lower bound (CRLB), can be eliminated, and the estimation of high-order motion parameters under different scenarios is analyzed. Finally, numerical experiments validate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
Introduction: As application demands continue to grow, the high-precision motion estimation of maneuverable targets in modern radar systems is increasingly emphasized [1]-[5]. With the advancement of high technology, there are more and more high-speed, high-maneuverability targets in the radar search field. Secondly, for radars equipped with phased array antennas [1], [2], digital beamforming (DBF) technology has been widely used to achieve long exposure times. Therefore, higher order motions such as acceleration and deceleration cannot be ignored to achieve long integration times [1], [2], [21]-[23], even for targets that move slowly over such a long observation period. Thirdly, it is crucial for high-resolution imaging radars, such as Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) [3] and Inverse Synthetic Aperture Radar (ISAR) [4], [5], to estimate and compensate for high-order non-cooperative motions to obtain focused images. Typically, the range to a maneuverable radar target can be modeled as a multivariate function of its illumination time and multiple motion parameters (such as velocity and acceleration). At the same time, the motion of the target modulates the envelope and phase of the coherent radar target echoes, allowing the radar to measure the target's motion. In this regard, extensive research has been conducted over the past decades. Bello [6] and Kelly [7] discussed some basic aspects of joint estimation of target distance, velocity, and acceleration in the 1960s. However, the relationship between measurement accuracy and radar parameters (such as waveform duration, bandwidth, and transmit power) could not be clearly described. For widely used radar linear frequency modulated pulse sequences, Abatzoglou [8], [9] derived the Cramer-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) for velocity and acceleration estimation based on maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) in the 1990s. Unfortunately, further discussion on motion estimation for maneuverable targets with higher order than acceleration was not provided. On the other hand, many literatures [10]-[20] have discussed maneuverable target motion estimation based on polynomial phase signal (PPS) models. However, these works often omit envelope modulation information and may not achieve final accuracy based on joint envelope and phase information in real applications.
In addition to the envelope and phase joint measurement of coherent radars, some radars can only estimate target motion based on partial information, i.e., only phase measurements. For applications limited to networks, there are two typical examples. Firstly, due to the unknown initial phase of each pulse generated by the transmitter magnetron, early non-coherent radars were contaminated with uncertain phase modulation, leaving only envelope information directly available for measuring target motion [1], [2]. Secondly, to overcome the unknownness of target translational motion, Inverse Synthetic Aperture Radar (ISAR) typically employs envelope lines for high-resolution range imaging (HRRP) [4], [5]. For pure phase radar applications, two typical examples are as follows. Firstly, Continuous Wave (CW) radars [1], [2] have been widely used for vehicle motion measurement based on Doppler information, i.e., phase measurement. Secondly, for search radars with extremely low range resolution, such as Over-The-Horizon (OTHR), targets generally do not exceed one range unit during the coherent accumulation time, and the echo envelope cannot reflect the target's motion. Therefore, only phase information, namely, Doppler frequency, is commonly used in OTHR to recover target motion.
Then, it is interesting to ask what the optimal method and inherent precision limitations are for kinematic radar target motion estimation under different circumstances. In this paper, we provide a unified framework for target motion estimation in three scenarios: envelope and phase joint measurement, pure phase measurement, and high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) cases with pure envelope measurement. Specifically, for maneuvering targets with arbitrary motions, we propose different signal models and corresponding maximum likelihood estimations (MLE), namely, generalized Radon-Fourier transform (GRFT) [21]-[23], generalized Radon transform (GRT), and generalized Fourier transform (GFT). Interestingly, the proposed GRFT has the same form as the likelihood ratio detector (LRT) [21]-[23] for maneuvering targets, which is a statistical optimal detector in the noise background. Moreover, the last two proposed MLEs, namely, GFT and GRT, are special cases of GRFT where only phase or only phase information is used. Then, using the proposed GRFT, an optimal estimator and detector can be unified for radar maneuvering target detection and parameter estimation. Additionally, by approximating the arbitrary multivariate range function of maneuvering targets as a higher order polynomial, the corresponding CRLB is obtained, and the performance analysis of high-order motion estimation is conducted. The contributions of this paper in the field of maneuvering target motion estimation are mainly reflected in four aspects. Firstly, a unified framework is proposed for the signal model, providing a common basis for three different radar scenarios. Secondly, the optimal MLE methods, namely, GRFT, GRT, and GFT, are derived from above. Thirdly, the CRLB is further obtained by approximating the range function as a higher order polynomial. Finally, some new analyses provide suggestions for the CRLB. The results show that, based on the given system parameters, using the proposed CRLB and radar equation, the inherent precision of high-order motion estimation can be analytically obtained.
